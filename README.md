# AI Game

Top-down 3D survival extraction looter shooter built in Godot 4 with GDScript. The design lives in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## Run it

1. Install **Godot 4.7** (standard version, not .NET) from https://godotengine.org/download. It's a single program file: unzip it and run it, no installer needed.
2. Get this repo: `git clone https://github.com/Tiddywagon/ai-game.git`, or **Code → Download ZIP** on GitHub.
3. In Godot's Project Manager, click **Import**, select this folder's `project.godot`, then **Import & Edit**.
4. Press **F5** (or the ▶ button, top right) to run. The game starts at the main menu.

## Playing

On first launch you pick a faction (BEAR or USEC: +0.05 starting reputation with Bulat or with Tinker); that creates your profile. BEAR starts with an AK-74N and Module-3M armor, USEC with a Kestrel AR and PACA armor (plus the same meds, food and grenades in the stash), and each looks and sounds like its faction in a raid. Experience from raids and quests raises your player level, 1 to 50 (level 10 takes about 10 good raids, 35 about 100): the main menu shows it top right with the XP toward the next level, the raid summary says "Level 11 → 12" when a raid took you up, and trader loyalty levels 2–4 need levels 15, 25 and 35. From the main menu:

- **ENTER RAID**: pick a map (Factory and Test Yard are playable so far; each shows its boss and how much of it you've explored), play as your PMC or as a Scav, check your loadout (EDIT LOADOUT opens the loadout screen to change it; insure it with Bulat or Doc Vera), then load in. A **Scav run** gives you a random kit (nothing from your stash at risk; loot you extract goes to the stash) and drops you into a raid already under way, 20–65% of the way through (never with less than 5 minutes left): AI PMCs have been roaming (some have left, a few lie dead at points of interest), raid events have already dropped their crates, and some of the loot is gone. Run Through counts from when you arrived (under 7 minutes, or under half the time that was left when you joined, whichever is shorter). After you deploy as a Scav the next Scav run is 20 real minutes away (SCAV and the main menu say "Scav ready in 12:30"); the kit is rolled once per cooldown. Your **Scav karma** (−6 to +6, Mole's loyalty) falls when you kill a friendly Scav as a Scav (−1, a boss −2; one that bleeds out from your shots counts, one that shot at you first or was out to shoot you for your low karma doesn't) and rises for each PMC you kill (+0.25), each extract (+0.1) and extracting with a friendly Scav nearby or just ahead of you through the same extract (+0.5): below −1 AI Scavs shoot you on sight, the kit gets worse at −2 and −4 and better at +2, +4 and +6 (with a shorter cooldown at +4 and +6), and Mole pays more, stocks better and slightly cheaper gear at higher loyalty (LL2 at +1, LL3 at +3, LL4 at +5) and charges more below 0. AI Scavs that see you shoot a Scav turn on you. The raid summary lists the changes. Insured gear you lose comes back to the stash 1–2 real minutes later (24–48 game hours), unless someone takes it off your body; the main menu counts down, and it arrives on time whatever menu screen you're on. Lab Zero takes a Lab Zero access keycard.
- **HIDEOUT**: a plan of the hideout with all 13 stations (Generator, Stash, Medstation, Nutrition Unit, Rest Space, Workbench, Water Collector, Lavatory, Shooting Range, Gym, Intelligence Center, Bitcoin Farm, Security) as rooms showing what each is doing, and a panel per station: hand items over as you find them (found in raid from level 2), build in real time (minutes to hours, also while the game is closed), queue crafts and collect them, fuel the generator (no power: half-speed crafts, no farm), fill the Bitcoin Farm with graphics cards, collect water, train at the Gym, shorten the Scav cooldown and cheapen insurance with the Intelligence Center, scout raids with Security, practise on the Shooting Range with nothing at stake, and buy cosmetics. The Stash starts at level 1 (a 10 × 30 stash); levels 2–4 grow it to 10 × 40, 10 × 50 and 10 × 70. Items the hideout or a quest still needs carry a small house icon in every grid.
- **LOADOUT** (also EDIT LOADOUT in the raid's loadout check, and LOADOUT or Tab on CHARACTER): your character and the stash side by side. Every slot (helmet, headset, face cover, body armor, rig, backpack, secure container, primary, secondary, pistol, melee), the pockets and the worn rig's, backpack's and secure container's grids; under the slots the **total stats** (armor class and durability over the head, face, thorax, stomach, arms and legs, weight against the 25 / 40 kg thresholds, carry limit and free cells, the gun in hand's ergonomics and recoil, hearing), live as you change things. Drag between the stash and your character; dropping on a filled slot swaps the two (the old item goes where the new one came from, or into the stash). **Hover** a stash item you could wear (or put the controller cursor on it, or drag it) to compare it with what you wear in its slot: green ▲ better, red ▼ worse. **Presets**: save up to 5 named loadouts (SAVE NEW, OVERWRITE, RENAME, DELETE); **LOAD** puts what you wear into the stash, then dresses you from the stash (the best match for each item, its parts, the round in the gun, what's in the pockets, rig and backpack) and lists what was missing; nothing is lost or made up (what has no room on you goes back where it was, a smaller magazine's spare rounds go to the stash, and if something would have nowhere to go nothing changes and it says so). **AUTO-EQUIP BEST GEAR** puts on the best you own from the stash: body armor and rig as a pair (an armored rig alone or armor with a plain rig, whichever is better), helmet and face cover as a pair (no mask under a helmet whose face shield already stops more), headset, backpack, melee and guns (scored by armor class first, then coverage and durability, less weight and lost hearing; room; the gun's handling with its parts plus the best round you own; rarity breaking ties; what you wear goes only for something clearly better), fits a magazine, loads each gun with the best rounds you own (from the stash, then from your pockets, rig and backpack; a gun left short says so), packs two magazines' worth of the best spare rounds (worse ones you carry go back to the stash for better ones) and a bandage, tourniquet, splint and medkit in your pockets or rig. Set a **budget per item** (empty: no limit; a gun counts with the magazine it gets) and **Protect Legendary** to leave Legendary items, rounds and meds alone (a gun with a Legendary part counts); both are saved. A summary lists every change and anything that had no room, with **UNDO** (puts back exactly what it moved; anything that reached the stash meanwhile stays, and what can't go back stays as it was and it says so) and **OK**. The secure container is never touched. Closing saves. The **QUICK BUY** tab (Q / E or LB / RB, or click it) buys a whole loadout in one go: ten cards from a pistol-and-bandages run to a class 5 assault kit (one only BEAR sees, one only USEC), each with its pieces, the stats you'd have, and its price at your current trader prices less 10% (so it gets cheaper as your loyalty rises; dollars where a line is The Broker's); a locked card says what it still needs ("Needs Bulat LL3 · 14 tasks completed (6 so far)"). If a trader is out of an item, the closest available one stands in (never a worse kind: a magazine as big, a sight of the same type, rounds as good at piercing while there are any; nothing like it, and the line is left out and not charged). BUY shows every line, each swap, where the rounds and meds go and what goes to the stash before you CONFIRM; buying puts what you wore into the stash (rigs and backpacks with what's in them; the secure container stays on), equips the kit with loaded magazines, and refuses with nothing paid if the stash has no room. **SAVE CURRENT GEAR AS QUICK-BUY** turns what you wear into a custom card priced from trader stock (up to 5; what no trader sells is left out and named). Loot you extract with stays on your character until you move it (a Scav run's loot goes straight to the stash). The stash has a filter bar: search by name ("ak74" finds the AK-74N), category and rarity pickers, **FIR only** and **CLEAR** dim what doesn't match (nothing moves; a backpack opened from the stash dims what's inside it the same way), and **SORT** repacks the stash by type, rarity, value, name or size (stacks merge into full stacks without losing a round; if it wouldn't fit, nothing changes).
- **CHARACTER**: stats (level, with the XP toward the next one; your Scav karma, with Mole's loyalty level, the Scav kit tier it gets you and the Scav cooldown counting down; health, money, Drip, trick shots, items in the stash) and skills; **LOADOUT** (or Tab) opens the loadout screen.
- **TRADERS**: seven traders, each with a role, a personality and a greeting: Bulat (weapons and ammo), Doc Vera (meds, stims, food), Auntie Zoya (general goods and cheap barters), Stitch (gear and armor), Tinker (weapon parts and mods), Mole (the fence: buys anything at a low price, sells worn gear off Scavs) and The Broker (high-end, in dollars; locked until Auntie Zoya's introduction quest, from player level 10). Loyalty levels and what the next one needs; BUY (only what your loyalty level reaches: rarer items need higher levels, and the list ends with how many more each level adds), SELL, BARTER and TASKS (hand-in quests) for each trader; AMMO compares rounds, Stitch upgrades gear, and Bulat and Doc Vera INSURE the gear you carry (one item or all of it). **SELL** lists what that trader buys from your stash, pockets, rig and backpack with its price: sell one, or tick several and SELL SELECTED; filter by name. Each trader pays a share of an item's worth (a worn gun or vest, a half-used medkit or a key with few uses left are worth less; upgraded gear more), more at higher loyalty, and sales count toward the money spent for loyalty; The Broker pays in dollars, Mole buys anything for 30% at loyalty level 1 (up to 39% as your Scav karma raises his loyalty, down to 18% at −6). Containers sell only empty, and nothing you wear or keep in the secure container is offered. With *Confirm before selling valuables* on (Settings > Gameplay), selling an Epic or Legendary item, a valuable or insured gear asks first (insured gear says so on its row). Examining an item in the inventory (out of raid) offers SELL to whoever pays most. **BARTER** trades items for items (155 barters in `data/traders/barters/`, at every trader; Mole swaps junk for worn Scav gear): each lists what it asks for with how many your stash holds and a green check or a red cross, what it gives (several things for some), how many trades are left this restock, or the quest that unlocks it. **FIND** shows your stash beside the list with those items lit up and the rest dimmed; **TRADE** works once everything is in the stash (barters only take from the stash, found-in-raid items where the checkmark says so, and only whole food, drink and meds). A barter whose rewards traders also sell costs less than selling what it asks for and buying the rewards. Rarer rewards need higher loyalty (the rest are counted, "5 more barters at LL2"), and every barter good and valuable, boss trophies included, buys something. Traders restock every hour of real time (also while the game is closed; "Restock in 42:10"): rare and strong offers sell only a few each restock ("2 left", then SOLD OUT), each trader wants 2-3 items that pay 50% more (the WANTED strip on SELL; their hover text says so), and Mole's worn gear changes (at his loyalty level 1 Common and Uncommon gear, rounds, meds and junk, never what a trader keeps for a higher loyalty level, never valuables; rarer and less worn at higher levels). The Broker's EXCHANGE buys and sells dollars (145 / 125 roubles; changing money doesn't count toward loyalty, spending the dollars with him does). Higher loyalty also lowers prices: 3% off at LL2, 6% at LL3, 9% at LL4 with every trader but Mole (the BUY tab shows it), though never so low that something could be sold on to a trader at a profit.
- **FLEA MARKET** (from player level 15; also from the TRADERS screen): a player market. **BROWSE** about 140 listings at a time from made-up sellers (a new batch every 30 real minutes, the same for everyone on the same clock, also while the game is closed; rarer items less often, Legendary almost never; worn guns and armor, guns sometimes with extra parts), with a search, category and rarity filters and a sort by price, time left or value for money; each shows its price with a trend arrow, the market price and the cheapest trader price beside it. Market prices drift hourly between ×0.8 and ×1.4 of an item's worth, your buying pushes a price up and your listing pushes it down (fading over hours), and no listing is ever cheaper than what a trader would pay for it plus 3%, so nothing bought there sells on at a profit; what you buy is never found in raid. **SELL** puts found-in-raid items from your stash up for a fee (5% of the price, more above market) in 3 slots at level 15 (+1 every 5 levels): at or under the market price they sell within minutes, a little over takes hours, over 1.3× never, and unsold ones come back after 12 hours, also while the game is closed. **MY LISTINGS** collects the money and the returns (the main menu says when something waits). Keys and keycards, secure containers, boss uniques, quest items and the top armor-piercing rounds can't be listed.
- **Settings** (gear icon, bottom right): graphics, audio, controls and key bindings, gameplay, interface and accessibility.

Keyboard and mouse, controller and touch all work at the same time, and on-screen prompts switch to whichever you used last (keys, Xbox / PlayStation / Nintendo buttons, or touch buttons). Touch controls are on by themselves on touch screens (Settings > Controls: Auto / On / Off); EDIT LAYOUT there lets you drag, resize and fade every on-screen button and save up to 3 layouts. Aim assist (controller and touch), vibration and haptics can be turned off there too.

**Inventory**: drag items to move them; the cells underneath turn green where the item fits, amber where it stacks or goes inside a rig or backpack, red where it can't go. **R** while dragging turns it; **Shift**-drag splits a stack in half (the mouse wheel changes how many); **right-click** examines (stats, rarity, value); **double-click** a rig, backpack or case to open it; **Ctrl-click** quick-moves an item to the stash (or the container you're searching) and back. With a controller a cursor moves cell by cell: **A** picks up and puts down, **X** quick-moves (turns while holding), **View** examines; up from the stash's top row it reaches the filter bar (**A** works a control, **B** leaves the search box). On touch: drag to move, **long-press** to examine, tap a **second finger** while dragging to turn, **pinch** the stash to zoom. Drop guns, armor, rigs and so on onto the equipment slots to wear them (onto a filled slot: the two swap). The headset and face cover have slots of their own: a headset works with or without a helmet, and a ballistic mask (Shchit class 3, Kremen-4 class 4) covers the face like a face shield (with both on, whichever stops more takes the round). Everything you carry has weight: from 25 kg you slow down and sprinting tires you faster; from 40 kg you're overweight and can't sprint or roll (thresholds in `data/config/inventory.tres`).

**Raids and found in raid.** Search containers (crates, toolboxes, jackets, weapon boxes, medical bags, safes, filing cabinets, PCs, server racks, chests of drawers, medical crates) and bodies with **F**: they open beside your inventory and their contents turn up one item at a time while open (small items faster; closing pauses). Drag what you want across, or drag gear straight off a body's slots, and pick up loose items on the ground. You start at a random spawn point. To get out, stand in an extract you can use for 7 seconds before the clock (top right) runs out: a ring fills around you, and walking out or getting hit starts it over. Then the raid fades out to the summary, and from there you go back to the hideout with everything saved. Every map has one to three extracts, at least one always open. Some are random (open only some raids: "Unknown" until you've seen it up close), some conditional: carry a key, pay roubles at the extract (F), throw the map's power switch, fire a signal flare in it (F), or leave your backpack behind. Some only appear once you've finished a quest. Your extracts show at the top right for a few seconds when the raid starts and whenever you press **O**, in the pause menu (Esc) and on the map item, each marked Open, Unknown, Closed or with what you still need. In the world each has a glowing ring and a sign in the same colours: green when you can use it, amber while something's missing, red when it's closed, grey while unknown. On the Test Yard: the north-east gate (always open), the smuggler's car (7,000 roubles, no backpack) and, after Bulat's Flare Path quest, the signal point (fire a flare). AI PMCs and some Scavs leave through the same extracts late in the raid. Anything you looted gets a faded checkmark; if you extract it turns solid: the item is **found in raid** (FIR). Dying, running out of time, LEAVE RAID, or a **Run Through** (extracting within 7 minutes with under 200 raid experience) gives nothing FIR; a Run Through also earns no experience, while extracting properly earns 300 more. As a PMC, dying or leaving loses everything except your secure container and **insured** gear: press INSURE GEAR in the loadout check, INSURE when examining an item, or use Bulat's or Doc Vera's INSURE tab at the traders to insure guns, armor, helmets, rigs and backpacks (Bulat 8% of their value and slower, Doc Vera 15% and faster), and what you lose comes back to your stash 1–2 real minutes later unless someone took it off your body. Your secure container (Alpha 2×2 to start; the Beta from the Workbench, the Gamma and Kappa from quests) is never lost. Every raid ends on a summary with your kills, the value of the loot you carried out (or lost), the experience you earned and where it came from, and what happened to your gear. Bought, bartered and reward items are never FIR; hideout crafts always are; what you bring in keeps its status. Quests, hideout upgrades and some barters only take FIR items where the requirement shows the checkmark. Hover a checkmark for "Found in raid"; the stash's **FIR only** filter dims the rest; examine a gun to see (or UNLOAD) its rounds by status.

**Factory** is a small gray-box factory for fast, close-quarters raids: a main hall with a catwalk up the stairs, a dark boiler house, offices, locker rooms, a med bay and lab, and a warehouse with an armory cage; a red smokestack, a yellow crane and a blue water tower help you find your way. **F** opens and closes doors (closed doors stop bullets and sight). Locked doors (red plate) need their key on you: the Factory office key opens the overseer's office above the main hall, the Factory lab key the lab; a key loses one use each time it unlocks a door. The office, the lab and the armory cage hold better loot than anywhere else. Extracts: **Gate 3** (south wall, always open), the **Cellars** (boiler house; carry the Factory exit key and throw the boiler house power switch) and the **Office window** (open only some raids). Eight Scavs patrol the main hall, warehouse and lockers; five AI PMCs (BEAR and USEC squads) play the raid like you do, three from the start and two arriving later, each squad with its own plan: a Rat sneaks and avoids fights, a Looter goes from one point of interest to the next, a Chad hunts gunfire, a Quester visits its objectives and leaves. They loot containers and bodies (leaving alone one you have open), heal after fights and head for an extract when time runs low or their bags are full of loot; their gear is better on harder maps. An open door doesn't stop them (it still stops you, sight and bullets). F12 pressed three times shows each one's plan and route (the Test Yard has three AI PMCs too, one arriving late). Carry the **Factory map** (Bulat sells it; it also turns up in filing cabinets and on the guard's desk) and press **M** to see the layout, where you are and your extracts with what each needs right now; examine it and press VIEW MAP to study it out of raid. The Test Yard has its own map.

**Scavs and bosses.** Scavs wander their zone (the Factory's main hall, warehouse and locker rooms; the Test Yard's north and east yards), walk over to footsteps they hear and run to gunshots, calling the Scavs of their zone within 20 m to come too. More arrive in waves every few minutes while fewer than 10 are alive (a boss and its guards don't count). Their guns are mostly cheap (a sawn-off, a revolver, an SKS, a pump shotgun, a Mosin or a pistol; sometimes an AK, rarely better), and most carry a small medkit they use when they run to heal. Each map's boss (35% of raids, with 2-4 guards in class 4-5 armor) always carries a Legendary unique found nowhere else (a gold whistle, a gilded wrench, a cash brick, a snow leopard pelt, a black pearl, a black-box drive) on top of Epic or Legendary loot; raid events don't draw a boss or its guards off their haunt, nor does a fight farther than 25 m from it (a closer one they come to).

**Factions and AI PMCs.** BEAR wear dark green with a red band round the head, USEC steel blue with an amber band (Scavs are tan). AI PMCs are BEAR or USEC and come in squads of 1 to 3 of one faction: the followers keep behind their leader, fight what any of them sees, go together to noises they hear, and leave the raid together through the extract the leader picks; when the leader falls the next one leads. They fight Scavs and every other PMC, but hold back from their own faction (you too): slower to open fire, and they leave one alone beyond 15 m unless it aims at them or shoots. PMCs call out what's happening (spotting you, being hit, reloading, a kill, a mate down, moving, healing, heading out) on the Voice bus, BEAR in Russian, USEC in English; you do too (reloading, a grenade out, being hit, a kill). Voices carry 30 m from you (the game listens from your character, not the camera). With Subtitles on (Settings > Interface) you see the lines of PMCs in sight and your own; a voice you can't see but can hear shows as a light blue sound marker, and AI within 8 m hear it too. If someone shoots one of a squad, the whole squad stops holding back from them. Faction data (looks, voice lines, starter kits, AI kits, squad and voice tuning) is in `data/config/factions.json`.

**Weapon modding** (out of raid): right-click a gun in the inventory and press **MODIFY**. Drag parts from the list on the right onto the gun's slots (a rail handguard adds slots for a foregrip, a laser and a sight); drag a part off, or double-click it, to take it off. Hover a part to see what it would do: the recoil pattern and spread circles show the change drawn over the gun as it is, and the stats show the new values, green if better, red if worse. Rarer parts have bigger bonuses (Common about 4%, Legendary about 23%), but every part adds weight, and heavy builds sway more and settle slower. You can also drop a part straight onto a gun in the inventory to fit it to a free slot. Guns wear with every shot; below 85 durability they start to jam (**R** clears a jam, "JAMMED" on the HUD). **REPAIR** in the modding screen fixes a gun for roubles, but lowers its max durability a little each time. Tinker sells parts.

Keys and controller buttons can be remapped in Settings > Controls. Menus work with the arrow keys, Enter and Esc, or the D-pad, A and B; Q / E or LB / RB switch settings tabs.

Test Yard debug keys: **F1** left leg fracture, **F2** overweight, **F3** next round type for the gun's caliber, **F4** AI on/off, **F5** heal everything, **F6** cycle the gun's sight (iron, red dot, holo, 2-4x, 6-8x), **F7** time of day, **F8** weather, **F9** flashbang at the reticle, **F10** fog of war off.

You only see inside a cone pointed at the reticle, plus a small circle around you; enemies and loot outside it aren't drawn, and everything else out of view stays just faintly visible in the dark (nothing you've seen before is remembered). Rounds fired at you glow faintly through the dark, so you see shots coming even when you can't see the shooter. Walls, cars, crates and the hedge block sight; low cover only blocks it while you crouch. Night, dark rooms, fog and rain shorten your sight; the flashlight helps. Sounds you can't see the source of show as arrows at the screen edge.

Health works per body part, as in Tarkov: head or thorax at 0 kills, any other part at 0 is "blacked" and passes further damage on to the rest of the body. Hits can cause bleeds and fractures. The silhouette in the bottom-left corner shows each part's health and effects. When you die, the death screen says what killed you.

**Meds, stims and food.** Bandages stop light bleeds, tourniquets heavy ones, splints set fractures, painkillers numb pain (and let you run on a broken leg), medkits heal from an HP pool (the bigger ones stop bleeds too, paying from the pool), surgical kits bring back a blacked limb at a cost to its max HP, and stimulants give a few minutes of buffs (regeneration, stamina, speed, carry weight, steady aim, faster reloads, longer sight, quieter steps, no bleeds or fractures, even blacked limbs restored) after a 0-5 s delay, followed by side effects (tremor, blurred vision, fatigue, hunger, thirst). 19 stims from Common to Legendary; they stack, but 3 within 5 minutes cause an Overdose (HP drain, tremor, blurred vision). Active stims show as icons with countdowns above the body on the HUD; examine one for every buff and side effect with its numbers. Find them in medicine and lab cabinets, medical bags and safes; Doc Vera sells cheap ones and barters the Epic and Legendary ones, and the Medstation crafts Common to Rare stims. Injecting them trains Stim resistance, which shortens side effects (tuning in `data/config/stims.json`). Everything takes time to use (a bandage 3 s, surgery 14 s), slows you to a walk, and is interrupted (nothing used up) if you shoot, roll, sprint, reload, aim, get hit or press E again. Press **E** to treat the worst problem with what's in your pockets, rig or secure container (the HUD shows what it would use), or examine any item you carry and press **USE**. Energy and hydration (0-100) drain during raids, hydration twice as fast: more while sprinting, rolling, overweight, injured or on hot maps (Shoreline Resort, Factory), less while crouched or standing still. Below 30 stamina recovers slower and the HUD warns you; at 0 Exhaustion or Dehydration drains HP from every body part, shakes your aim and blurs your view. They carry over between raids and refill slowly in the hideout (faster with the Nutrition Unit and Rest Space built); eat and drink from the stash. Food and drink take a few seconds, can be cancelled, and multi-use items (water, jerky, field rations) keep their uses left. Hover a food or drink in the inventory to see what one use does ("Energy 62 → 77 (+15)"). Crackers, sugar and jerky give energy but cost water; vodka and moonshine kill pain but dry you out and shake your hands; Field ration packs and Survivor's emergency kits restore both. Look for food in fridges, kitchen cabinets, store shelves, gas station counters and on Scavs; the Nutrition Unit cooks it from junk (water + sugar + coffee = energy drinks). Eating and drinking train the Metabolism skill, which slows the drain. The best meds (AFAK, Surv12, Grizzly, eTG-c) and valuables (Roler, coin collection, crypto drive, LEDX) are Epic or Legendary; barter goods (batteries, toothpaste, cigarettes and more) go into barters, hideout crafts and upgrades. F5 heals and refills you while testing.

**Barter goods and valuables.** Every piece of junk and every valuable has a group (Hardware, Tools, Electronics, Tech, Household, Jewelry, Currency, Collectibles, Intel, Medical supply, Fuel, Military, Materials) and tags (electronics, flammable, jewelry, valuable...), and is worth a range set by its rarity: Common 5k-25k roubles up to a million and more for Legendaries (the jeweled egg, crypto drive, LEDX, tank battery). Containers roll by group: toolboxes hold hardware and tools, PCs and server racks electronics and tech, jackets and chests of drawers household goods, jewelry and money, medical crates medical supplies, and safes, weapon boxes and boss bodies anything valuable. Examine an item to see its group, tags and what it's **Needed for**: hover that line for every quest, hideout upgrade, barter, craft and gear upgrade that takes it (a barter you can't make yet names the loyalty level or quest it waits for; hovering an item in the inventory sums it up). Groups, tags and value bands are in `data/config/item_groups.json`.

**Guns and ammo.** There are 25 guns (pistols, SMGs, shotguns, assault rifles, DMRs, bolt-action rifles and machine guns; two are Common junk that Scavs carry, a sawn-off and a revolver, with no parts to fit) in 10 calibers, each with 3-5 kinds of round: cheap everyday rounds, hard-hitting hollow points that stop at armor, and rare armor-piercing ones (DESIGN.md §6.4 has the tables). Drag rounds onto a gun to load them; if you run out of the gun's round type, reloading switches to another one of its caliber you carry. Armor matters: helmets and vests stop rounds on the parts they cover depending on the round's penetration against their class (and wear down as they're hit), so soft rounds wreck unarmored targets while armor-piercing ones get through plates for less damage. Each round also has its own velocity (range and damage falloff), recoil and accuracy changes, fragmentation, ricochet and bleed chances; tracers show where you fire, to everyone. Gear comes in armor classes 1-6: vests, plate carriers, armored rigs and helmets, each covering certain body parts (helmets don't cover the face: fit a face shield or wear a ballistic mask for that; a headset, in its own slot, lets you hear further). Armored rigs can't be worn with body armor. Gear can be upgraded +1 to +5 (rarer gear goes higher) at Stitch or the hideout Workbench for roubles and barter items: armor gets more durability, rigs and backpacks more room and less weight, headsets better hearing. Examine an item and press UPGRADE. Examine a gun or a round, or open a trader's AMMO tab, for a chart comparing every round of the caliber against armor classes 1-6. Traders sell better rounds at higher loyalty levels; the best armor-piercing rounds are only found in raid, made at the Workbench or bartered for Legendary valuables at loyalty level 4. Buckshot fires 8 pellets; chokes tighten them. Every other gun has its own parts to mod it with, from Common to Legendary, and many parts fit several guns (AK grips on the Tundra 47, STANAG magazines in every 5.56 gun).

**360 trick shots.** With a bolt-action rifle (Mosin, Huntsman 700, Leviathan .338) or DMR (SVD, Partisan, Longbow DMR) in hand, sweep the reticle a full circle around your character in one direction within 1.5 seconds, rolling or not. A gold ring fills around the reticle as you go, and when it closes **TRICK SHOT READY** shows for 1 second: the first shot in that window deals +50% damage. You can't aim while spinning, so it's a hip shot with full bloom. A trick-shot hit gets a gold marker, a sting and "360!"; a kill gets a banner, bonus experience and Drip. The character screen counts your trick shots and Drip, and Bulat, Tinker and Auntie Zoya have trick-shot quests. Spin time, window and bonus are in each gun's file in `data/weapons/` (Trick shot); the XP and Drip in `data/config/raid.tres`.

**Recoil and bloom.** Every shot climbs (the reticle kicks out along your aim and rounds fly higher, so long bursts at range go over heads) and kicks a little left or right; it pulls back by itself after each shot, and you can fight the side kick with the mouse. The reticle is a circle the size of your current spread: it grows with every shot and while you move, sprint, roll or fire from the hip, and shrinks back when you stop. Hold right-click to raise the sight and lose the hip-fire spread. Heavy builds sway more and settle slower; worn guns spread more.

The ammo count, spare rounds and reload progress are in the bottom-right corner. With **Auto-reload** on (Settings > Gameplay), pulling the trigger on an empty magazine reloads.

The traders (names, roles, personalities, greetings, how they unlock, how often they restock and what they buy when you sell) live in `data/traders/traders.json`, their offers (ids, prices, loyalty levels, stock per restock) in `data/traders/offers.json`, the rules every trader shares (the rarity each loyalty level unlocks, what makes an item worth more or less, Wanted items, the exchange rates, the resale floor under discounts, Mole's worn gear) in `data/config/trading.json`, the flea market (unlock level, listings, price drift and pressure, fees, slots, sale times, banned items) in `data/config/flea.json`, the quick-buy cards in `data/quick_buy/` (one file per card: its unlock and each item with the trader it's bought from) and their rules (bundle discount, how alternatives are chosen) in `data/config/quick_buy.json`, quests in `data/quests/quests.json`, the player level curve and what each trader loyalty level needs in `data/config/progression.json`, Scav runs (when a Scav joins the raid, the fast-forward, the cooldown, karma and the Scav kit tiers) in `data/config/scav_runs.json`, hideout stations and crafts in `data/hideout/stations.json` (requirements are `{"item", "count", "fir"}`; a level can also need other stations, `"stations": {"generator": 1}`, and trader loyalty, `"traders": {"bulat": 2}`; the Stash station's levels set the stash's size, `"stash_size": [10, 40]`), and extraction, Run Through and raid experience in `data/config/raid.tres`. Roll, cover and bullet numbers live in `data/config/combat.tres`; body-part HP, bleed and fracture chances in `data/config/health.tres`; energy, hydration, drain multipliers, hideout refill and item-use tuning in `data/config/consumables.tres` (each map's climate in its `data/maps/` file) (what each med, stim and food does is in its item's `stats`: `use_time`, `uses` or `hp`, `treats`, `costs`, `effects`, `energy`, `hydration`); every vision angle, range and multiplier (cones, light, weather, optics, AI) in `data/config/vision.tres`. Each gun's handling (fire rate, magazine, reload times, recoil, spread and bloom, sway), its mod slots and default parts, and how it wears and jams are a `WeaponData` file in `data/weapons/`; how strong parts are by rarity and what ergonomics, weight, wear and skills do are in `data/config/handling.tres`, and each attachment kind's default effects in `data/config/attachment_profiles.json`; add a gun by adding a file there and pointing its item (in `data/items/weapons/`) at it. Rounds are in `data/items/ammo/<caliber>.json` (damage, penetration, armor damage, fragmentation, velocity, recoil, accuracy, ricochet, bleed chances, and pellets or tracer colour), armor stats in `data/items/armor/` and `data/items/helmets/` (face shields and headsets in `data/items/attachments/helmet_mods.json`, face covers in `data/items/face_covers/`), gear upgrades in `data/config/gear_upgrades.json`, the armor rules in `data/config/combat.tres` (Rounds and armor), weapon parts in `data/items/attachments/`. Your settings are saved in `settings.cfg` and your profile in `savegame.json`, both in Godot's user folder (on Windows: `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\AI Game\`).

## Adding items

Every item lives in `data/items/` (any subfolder). No code needed: either make an `ItemData` resource in the Inspector and save it as a `.tres`, or add an entry to a `.json` file (one item, or a list):

```json
{
  "id": "gas_analyzer",
  "name": "Gas analyzer",
  "description": "Wanted for the Medstation.",
  "category": "junk",
  "rarity": "uncommon",
  "size": [1, 2],
  "weight": 0.6,
  "value": 25000,
  "max_stack": 1,
  "stats": {},
  "loot_weight": 1.0
}
```

Only `id`, `name`, `category` and `rarity` are required. Categories: weapon, ammo, attachment, armor, rig, backpack, helmet, meds, food, valuable, junk, key, container, melee, throwable, face_cover. Rarities: common, uncommon, rare, ultra_rare (or "Ultra Rare"), epic, legendary. Guns add `"weapon": "res://data/weapons/<gun>.tres"`; `"icon": "res://..."` replaces the placeholder icon; `"loot_weight": 0` keeps an item out of random loot. Rigs, backpacks and cases get their own grid with `"stats": {"grid": [4, 2]}` (add `"secure": true` for a secure container); pistols need `"stats": {"class": "pistol"}` to go in the pistol slot. Mistakes show up in the Output panel with the file name.

Weapon parts are attachments with a `"mod"` stat (see `data/items/attachments/`):

```json
{"id": "ak_compensator", "name": "AK compensator", "category": "attachment", "rarity": "uncommon", "weight": 0.15, "value": 6000,
 "stats": {"mod": {"kind": "muzzle", "fits": ["ak"], "handling": {"vertical_recoil": 1.3, "horizontal_recoil": 0.8}, "modifiers": {"noise": 10}}}}
```

`kind` is one of muzzle, barrel, handguard, foregrip, stock, pistol_grip, receiver, sight, tactical, magazine; `fits` lists gun platforms (`WeaponData.platform`; leave it out for rail parts that fit any gun). A part does what its kind's profile in `data/config/attachment_profiles.json` says; `handling` replaces single entries of it: vertical_recoil, horizontal_recoil, recoil_recovery, base_spread, bloom_per_shot, max_bloom, bloom_recovery, hip_spread, ergonomics, weight. A positive value is a bonus that many times the rarity strength (Common 4% ... Legendary 23%), a negative value a fixed downside in % (ergonomics: points). `modifiers` are fixed effects on velocity, aim_speed, noise, malfunction and durability_loss (percent). `"standard": true` marks the stock part a gun comes with (no effects). Magazines add `"capacity": 30`; sights add `"optic"` at the top of `stats`; parts with rails add `"slots": [{"id": "foregrip", "name": "Foregrip", "kinds": ["foregrip"]}]`.

## Loot

Every container type has its own loot table in `data/loot_tables/` (`crate.tres`, `medical_bag.tres`, `safe.tres`...; loose loot uses `loose_loot.tres`). A roll picks a rarity (base weights Common 55, Uncommon 25, Rare 12, Ultra Rare 5, Epic 2.5, Legendary 0.5) multiplied by the table's own rarity multipliers (a safe boosts rare drops) and the map's (`loot_multipliers` in `data/maps/*.tres`, e.g. Lab Zero doubles Epic and Legendary), the point of interest it stands in (its tier and focus tags, `data/config/pois.json`) and any high-value loot area (`LootZone`), then an item of that rarity the table holds: its item list, categories, item groups (`groups`) and tags (`tags`), all together. Container types (`data/loot_tables/containers/`) set the table, grid size, search time and look; place one in a map with a `LootBox` node. Search timing is in `data/config/raid.tres`. To check the rates, open `scenes/debug/loot_roll_test.tscn` and press **F6** (Run Current Scene): it rolls 10,000 times for each setup and shows expected vs rolled rates, also printed to the Output panel. From a terminal: `godot --path . res://scenes/debug/loot_roll_test.tscn -- --quit --seed=42`.

## Maps

Each raid map is a scene in `maps/` whose root runs `scripts/maps/raid_level.gd` (it bakes the AI navmesh on load and handles death, the raid summary and the map key) and whose `MapInfo` in `data/maps/` points at it (`scene_path`; maps without one show "in development"). Build them from the gray-box nodes in `scripts/world/`, which all show in the editor: `CoverBox` (walls, props and cover: size and bullet material), `Door` (width, `locked` and `key_id`, a name), `Stairs` and `FloorSlab` (upper floors and catwalks), `MapLabel` (area names painted on the floor, landmarks), `LootBox` / `LootPickup` (containers and loose loot), `LootZone` (a box whose `rarity_multipliers` boost every roll inside it), `ScavZone` (AI Scavs spawned at its `Marker3D` children, which they wander between; a Scav placed in the scene joins a zone through its `zone` property; Scav waves spawn there too), `PointOfInterest` (a named area from `data/config/pois.json`: its tier, focus tags and requirement decide which spawn points inside it spawn and what they roll; `EventSpot_airdrop` / `_truck` / `_cache` markers inside it are where raid events land), `Lever` and `FloorHatch` (a hidden stash), `BossSpawn` (the map's boss from `data/config/bosses.json`, 35% of raids, with guards at its `Marker3D` children), `SpawnPoint`, `ExtractZone` (set its `extract_id`: the rules are the map's row in `data/config/extracts.json`, 1 to 3 extracts with at least one always open; the game warns if the table breaks that) and `PowerSwitch`. AI PMCs come by the map's schedule in `data/config/pmc_ai.json` (its budget, late arrivals, personalities, quest POIs and gear tiers, with `difficulty` in the map's `MapInfo`) at the map's PMC `SpawnPoint`s: don't place them in the scene. A map item is a junk item with `"stats": {"type": "map", "map": "<map id>"}`; the map screen is drawn from the scene itself, so it stays right as the map changes. `maps/factory.tscn` is written by `tools/make_factory_map.py` (`python3 tools/make_factory_map.py .`), but it's an ordinary scene: edit it in the editor, or change the generator and run it again (that overwrites editor changes).

## Notes

- Commit the `.uid` files Godot creates next to scripts and scenes; they keep references stable.
- `.godot/` is a local cache and is git-ignored.
- Placeholder music, UI sounds, effects and the factions' voice barks are generated by `tools/make_placeholder_audio.py` (needs Python, numpy and ffmpeg; add `voice` after the folder to make only the voice barks).
- When you set up an export preset, add `*.json` to its "Filters to export non-resource files" so JSON items are included.
