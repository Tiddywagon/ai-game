# AI Game

Top-down 3D survival extraction looter shooter built in Godot 4 with GDScript. The design lives in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## Run it

1. Install **Godot 4.7** (standard version, not .NET) from https://godotengine.org/download. It's a single program file: unzip it and run it, no installer needed.
2. Get this repo: `git clone https://github.com/Tiddywagon/ai-game.git`, or **Code → Download ZIP** on GitHub.
3. In Godot's Project Manager, click **Import**, select this folder's `project.godot`, then **Import & Edit**.
4. Press **F5** (or the ▶ button, top right) to run. The game starts at the main menu.

## Playing

On first launch you pick a faction (BEAR or USEC); that creates your profile. From the main menu:

- **ENTER RAID**: pick a map (Factory and Test Yard are playable so far; each shows its boss and how much of it you've explored), play as your PMC or as a Scav, check your loadout (and insure it with Prapor or Therapist), then load in. Insured gear you lose comes back to the stash 1–2 real minutes later (24–48 game hours), unless someone takes it off your body; the main menu counts down, and it arrives on time whatever menu screen you're on. Lab Zero takes a Lab Zero access keycard.
- **HIDEOUT**: build and upgrade stations (Generator, Medstation, Workbench, Lavatory, Nutrition Unit, Rest Space) and craft with them.
- **CHARACTER**: your gear, stats, skills and stash. **INVENTORY** (or Tab) opens the grid inventory with the stash beside it, to move gear between them.
- **TRADERS**: loyalty levels and what the next one needs; BUY, BARTER and TASKS (hand-in quests) for each trader; AMMO compares rounds, Ragman upgrades gear, and Prapor and Therapist INSURE the gear you carry (one item or all of it).
- **Settings** (gear icon, bottom right): graphics, audio, controls and key bindings, gameplay, interface and accessibility.

Keyboard and mouse, controller and touch all work at the same time, and on-screen prompts switch to whichever you used last (keys, Xbox / PlayStation / Nintendo buttons, or touch buttons). Touch controls are on by themselves on touch screens (Settings > Controls: Auto / On / Off); EDIT LAYOUT there lets you drag, resize and fade every on-screen button and save up to 3 layouts. Aim assist (controller and touch), vibration and haptics can be turned off there too.

**Inventory**: drag items to move them; the cells underneath turn green where the item fits, amber where it stacks or goes inside a rig or backpack, red where it can't go. **R** while dragging turns it; **Shift**-drag splits a stack in half (the mouse wheel changes how many); **right-click** examines (stats, rarity, value); **double-click** a rig, backpack or case to open it; **Ctrl-click** quick-moves an item to the stash (or the container you're searching) and back. With a controller a cursor moves cell by cell: **A** picks up and puts down, **X** quick-moves (turns while holding), **View** examines. On touch: drag to move, **long-press** to examine, tap a **second finger** while dragging to turn, **pinch** the stash to zoom. Drop guns, armor, rigs and so on onto the equipment slots to wear them. Everything you carry has weight: from 25 kg you slow down and sprinting tires you faster; from 40 kg you're overweight and can't sprint or roll (thresholds in `data/config/inventory.tres`).

**Raids and found in raid.** Search containers (crates, toolboxes, jackets, weapon boxes, medical bags, safes, filing cabinets, PCs, server racks, chests of drawers, medical crates) and bodies with **F**: they open beside your inventory and their contents turn up one item at a time while open (small items faster; closing pauses). Drag what you want across, or drag gear straight off a body's slots, and pick up loose items on the ground. You start at a random spawn point. To get out, stand in an extract you can use for 7 seconds before the clock (top right) runs out: a ring fills around you, and walking out or getting hit starts it over. Then the raid fades out to the summary, and from there you go back to the hideout with everything saved. Every map has one to three extracts, at least one always open. Some are random (open only some raids: "Unknown" until you've seen it up close), some conditional: carry a key, pay roubles at the extract (F), throw the map's power switch, fire a signal flare in it (F), or leave your backpack behind. Some only appear once you've finished a quest. Your extracts show at the top right for a few seconds when the raid starts and whenever you press **O**, in the pause menu (Esc) and on the map item, each marked Open, Unknown, Closed or with what you still need. In the world each has a glowing ring and a sign in the same colours: green when you can use it, amber while something's missing, red when it's closed, grey while unknown. On the Test Yard: the north-east gate (always open), the smuggler's car (7,000 roubles, no backpack) and, after Prapor's Flare Path quest, the signal point (fire a flare). AI PMCs and some Scavs leave through the same extracts late in the raid. Anything you looted gets a faded checkmark; if you extract it turns solid: the item is **found in raid** (FIR). Dying, running out of time, LEAVE RAID, or a **Run Through** (extracting within 7 minutes with under 200 raid experience) gives nothing FIR; a Run Through also earns no experience, while extracting properly earns 300 more. As a PMC, dying or leaving loses everything except your secure container and **insured** gear: press INSURE GEAR in the loadout check, INSURE when examining an item, or use Prapor's or Therapist's INSURE tab at the traders to insure guns, armor, helmets, rigs and backpacks (Prapor 8% of their value and slower, Therapist 15% and faster), and what you lose comes back to your stash 1–2 real minutes later unless someone took it off your body. Your secure container (Alpha 2×2 to start; the Beta from the Workbench, the Gamma and Kappa from quests) is never lost. Every raid ends on a summary with your kills, the value of the loot you carried out (or lost), the experience you earned and where it came from, and what happened to your gear. Bought, bartered and reward items are never FIR; hideout crafts always are; what you bring in keeps its status. Quests, hideout upgrades and some barters only take FIR items where the requirement shows the checkmark. Hover a checkmark for "Found in raid"; the stash's **FIR only** filter dims the rest; examine a gun to see (or UNLOAD) its rounds by status.

**Factory** is a small gray-box factory for fast, close-quarters raids: a main hall with a catwalk up the stairs, a dark boiler house, offices, locker rooms, a med bay and lab, and a warehouse with an armory cage; a red smokestack, a yellow crane and a blue water tower help you find your way. **F** opens and closes doors (closed doors stop bullets and sight). Locked doors (red plate) need their key on you: the Factory office key opens the overseer's office above the main hall, the Factory lab key the lab; a key loses one use each time it unlocks a door. The office, the lab and the armory cage hold better loot than anywhere else. Extracts: **Gate 3** (south wall, always open), the **Cellars** (boiler house; carry the Factory exit key and throw the boiler house power switch) and the **Office window** (open only some raids). Eight Scavs patrol the main hall, warehouse and lockers, and three AI PMCs spawn with you. Carry the **Factory map** (Prapor sells it; it also turns up in filing cabinets and on the guard's desk) and press **M** to see the layout, where you are and your extracts with what each needs right now; examine it and press VIEW MAP to study it out of raid. The Test Yard has its own map.

**Weapon modding** (out of raid): right-click a gun in the inventory and press **MODIFY**. Drag parts from the list on the right onto the gun's slots (a rail handguard adds slots for a foregrip, a laser and a sight); drag a part off, or double-click it, to take it off. Hover a part to see what it would do: the recoil pattern and spread circles show the change drawn over the gun as it is, and the stats show the new values, green if better, red if worse. Rarer parts have bigger bonuses (Common about 4%, Legendary about 23%), but every part adds weight, and heavy builds sway more and settle slower. You can also drop a part straight onto a gun in the inventory to fit it to a free slot. Guns wear with every shot; below 85 durability they start to jam (**R** clears a jam, "JAMMED" on the HUD). **REPAIR** in the modding screen fixes a gun for roubles, but lowers its max durability a little each time. Mechanic and Skier sell parts.

Keys and controller buttons can be remapped in Settings > Controls. Menus work with the arrow keys, Enter and Esc, or the D-pad, A and B; Q / E or LB / RB switch settings tabs.

Test Yard debug keys: **F1** left leg fracture, **F2** overweight, **F3** next round type for the gun's caliber, **F4** AI on/off, **F5** heal everything, **F6** cycle the gun's sight (iron, red dot, holo, 2-4x, 6-8x), **F7** time of day, **F8** weather, **F9** flashbang at the reticle, **F10** fog of war off.

You only see inside a cone pointed at the reticle, plus a small circle around you; enemies and loot outside it aren't drawn, and everything else out of view stays just faintly visible in the dark (nothing you've seen before is remembered). Rounds fired at you glow faintly through the dark, so you see shots coming even when you can't see the shooter. Walls, cars, crates and the hedge block sight; low cover only blocks it while you crouch. Night, dark rooms, fog and rain shorten your sight; the flashlight helps. Sounds you can't see the source of show as arrows at the screen edge.

Health works per body part, as in Tarkov: head or thorax at 0 kills, any other part at 0 is "blacked" and passes further damage on to the rest of the body. Hits can cause bleeds and fractures. The silhouette in the bottom-left corner shows each part's health and effects. When you die, the death screen says what killed you.

**Meds, stims and food.** Bandages stop light bleeds, tourniquets heavy ones, splints set fractures, painkillers numb pain (and let you run on a broken leg), medkits heal from an HP pool (the bigger ones stop bleeds too, paying from the pool), surgical kits bring back a blacked limb at a cost to its max HP, and stimulants give a few minutes of buffs (regeneration, stamina, speed, carry weight, steady aim, faster reloads, longer sight, quieter steps, no bleeds or fractures, even blacked limbs restored) after a 0-5 s delay, followed by side effects (tremor, blurred vision, fatigue, hunger, thirst). 19 stims from Common to Legendary; they stack, but 3 within 5 minutes cause an Overdose (HP drain, tremor, blurred vision). Active stims show as icons with countdowns above the body on the HUD; examine one for every buff and side effect with its numbers. Find them in medicine and lab cabinets, medical bags and safes; the Therapist sells cheap ones and barters the Epic and Legendary ones, and the Medstation crafts Common to Rare stims. Injecting them trains Stim resistance, which shortens side effects (tuning in `data/config/stims.json`). Everything takes time to use (a bandage 3 s, surgery 14 s), slows you to a walk, and is interrupted (nothing used up) if you shoot, roll, sprint, reload, aim, get hit or press E again. Press **E** to treat the worst problem with what's in your pockets, rig or secure container (the HUD shows what it would use), or examine any item you carry and press **USE**. Energy and hydration (0-100) drain during raids, hydration twice as fast: more while sprinting, rolling, overweight, injured or on hot maps (Shoreline Resort, Factory), less while crouched or standing still. Below 30 stamina recovers slower and the HUD warns you; at 0 Exhaustion or Dehydration drains HP from every body part, shakes your aim and blurs your view. They carry over between raids and refill slowly in the hideout (faster with the Nutrition Unit and Rest Space built); eat and drink from the stash. Food and drink take a few seconds, can be cancelled, and multi-use items (water, jerky, field rations) keep their uses left. Hover a food or drink in the inventory to see what one use does ("Energy 62 → 77 (+15)"). Crackers, sugar and jerky give energy but cost water; vodka and moonshine kill pain but dry you out and shake your hands; Field ration packs and Survivor's emergency kits restore both. Look for food in fridges, kitchen cabinets, store shelves, gas station counters and on Scavs; the Nutrition Unit cooks it from junk (water + sugar + coffee = energy drinks). Eating and drinking train the Metabolism skill, which slows the drain. The best meds (AFAK, Surv12, Grizzly, eTG-c) and valuables (Roler, coin collection, crypto drive, LEDX) are Epic or Legendary; barter goods (batteries, toothpaste, cigarettes and more) go into barters, hideout crafts and upgrades. F5 heals and refills you while testing.

**Barter goods and valuables.** Every piece of junk and every valuable has a group (Hardware, Tools, Electronics, Tech, Household, Jewelry, Currency, Collectibles, Intel, Medical supply, Fuel, Military, Materials) and tags (electronics, flammable, jewelry, valuable...), and is worth a range set by its rarity: Common 5k-25k roubles up to a million and more for Legendaries (the jeweled egg, crypto drive, LEDX, tank battery). Containers roll by group: toolboxes hold hardware and tools, PCs and server racks electronics and tech, jackets and chests of drawers household goods, jewelry and money, medical crates medical supplies, and safes, weapon boxes and boss bodies anything valuable. Examine an item to see its group, tags and what it's **Needed for**: hover that line for every quest, hideout upgrade, barter, craft and gear upgrade that takes it (hovering an item in the inventory sums it up). Groups, tags and value bands are in `data/config/item_groups.json`.

**Guns and ammo.** There are 22 guns (pistols, SMGs, shotguns, assault rifles, DMRs, bolt-action rifles and machine guns) in 10 calibers, each with 3-5 kinds of round: cheap everyday rounds, hard-hitting hollow points that stop at armor, and rare armor-piercing ones (DESIGN.md §6.4 has the tables). Drag rounds onto a gun to load them; if you run out of the gun's round type, reloading switches to another one of its caliber you carry. Armor matters: helmets and vests stop rounds on the parts they cover depending on the round's penetration against their class (and wear down as they're hit), so soft rounds wreck unarmored targets while armor-piercing ones get through plates for less damage. Each round also has its own velocity (range and damage falloff), recoil and accuracy changes, fragmentation, ricochet and bleed chances; tracers show where you fire, to everyone. Gear comes in armor classes 1-6: vests, plate carriers, armored rigs and helmets, each covering certain body parts (helmets don't cover the face: fit a face shield for that; a headset on the helmet lets you hear further). Armored rigs can't be worn with body armor. Gear can be upgraded +1 to +5 (rarer gear goes higher) at Ragman or the hideout Workbench for roubles and barter items: armor gets more durability, rigs and backpacks more room and less weight, headsets better hearing. Examine an item and press UPGRADE. Examine a gun or a round, or open a trader's AMMO tab, for a chart comparing every round of the caliber against armor classes 1-6. Traders sell better rounds at higher loyalty levels; the best armor-piercing rounds are only found in raid or made at the Workbench. Buckshot fires 8 pellets; chokes tighten them. Every gun has its own parts to mod it with, from Common to Legendary, and many parts fit several guns (AK grips on the Tundra 47, STANAG magazines in every 5.56 gun).

**360 trick shots.** With a bolt-action rifle (Mosin, Huntsman 700, Leviathan .338) or DMR (SVD, Partisan, Longbow DMR) in hand, sweep the reticle a full circle around your character in one direction within 1.5 seconds, rolling or not. A gold ring fills around the reticle as you go, and when it closes **TRICK SHOT READY** shows for 1 second: the first shot in that window deals +50% damage. You can't aim while spinning, so it's a hip shot with full bloom. A trick-shot hit gets a gold marker, a sting and "360!"; a kill gets a banner, bonus experience and Drip. The character screen counts your trick shots and Drip, and Prapor, Mechanic and Skier have trick-shot quests. Spin time, window and bonus are in each gun's file in `data/weapons/` (Trick shot); the XP and Drip in `data/config/raid.tres`.

**Recoil and bloom.** Every shot climbs (the reticle kicks out along your aim and rounds fly higher, so long bursts at range go over heads) and kicks a little left or right; it pulls back by itself after each shot, and you can fight the side kick with the mouse. The reticle is a circle the size of your current spread: it grows with every shot and while you move, sprint, roll or fire from the hip, and shrinks back when you stop. Hold right-click to raise the sight and lose the hip-fire spread. Heavy builds sway more and settle slower; worn guns spread more.

The ammo count, spare rounds and reload progress are in the bottom-right corner. With **Auto-reload** on (Settings > Gameplay), pulling the trigger on an empty magazine reloads.

Trader offers live in `data/traders/offers.json`, quests in `data/quests/quests.json`, hideout stations and crafts in `data/hideout/stations.json` (requirements are `{"item", "count", "fir"}`), and extraction, Run Through and raid experience in `data/config/raid.tres`. Roll, cover and bullet numbers live in `data/config/combat.tres`; body-part HP, bleed and fracture chances in `data/config/health.tres`; energy, hydration, drain multipliers, hideout refill and item-use tuning in `data/config/consumables.tres` (each map's climate in its `data/maps/` file) (what each med, stim and food does is in its item's `stats`: `use_time`, `uses` or `hp`, `treats`, `costs`, `effects`, `energy`, `hydration`); every vision angle, range and multiplier (cones, light, weather, optics, AI) in `data/config/vision.tres`. Each gun's handling (fire rate, magazine, reload times, recoil, spread and bloom, sway), its mod slots and default parts, and how it wears and jams are a `WeaponData` file in `data/weapons/`; how strong parts are by rarity and what ergonomics, weight, wear and skills do are in `data/config/handling.tres`, and each attachment kind's default effects in `data/config/attachment_profiles.json`; add a gun by adding a file there and pointing its item (in `data/items/weapons/`) at it. Rounds are in `data/items/ammo/<caliber>.json` (damage, penetration, armor damage, fragmentation, velocity, recoil, accuracy, ricochet, bleed chances, and pellets or tracer colour), armor stats in `data/items/armor/` and `data/items/helmets/` (face shields and headsets in `data/items/attachments/helmet_mods.json`), gear upgrades in `data/config/gear_upgrades.json`, the armor rules in `data/config/combat.tres` (Rounds and armor), weapon parts in `data/items/attachments/`. Your settings are saved in `settings.cfg` and your profile in `savegame.json`, both in Godot's user folder (on Windows: `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\AI Game\`).

## Adding items

Every item lives in `data/items/` (any subfolder). No code needed: either make an `ItemData` resource in the Inspector and save it as a `.tres`, or add an entry to a `.json` file (one item, or a list):

```json
{
  "id": "gas_analyzer",
  "name": "Gas analyzer",
  "description": "Wanted for the Medstation.",
  "category": "junk",
  "rarity": "uncommon",
  "size": [1, 2],
  "weight": 0.6,
  "value": 25000,
  "max_stack": 1,
  "stats": {},
  "loot_weight": 1.0
}
```

Only `id`, `name`, `category` and `rarity` are required. Categories: weapon, ammo, attachment, armor, rig, backpack, helmet, meds, food, valuable, junk, key, container, melee. Rarities: common, uncommon, rare, ultra_rare (or "Ultra Rare"), epic, legendary. Guns add `"weapon": "res://data/weapons/<gun>.tres"`; `"icon": "res://..."` replaces the placeholder icon; `"loot_weight": 0` keeps an item out of random loot. Rigs, backpacks and cases get their own grid with `"stats": {"grid": [4, 2]}` (add `"secure": true` for a secure container); pistols need `"stats": {"class": "pistol"}` to go in the pistol slot. Mistakes show up in the Output panel with the file name.

Weapon parts are attachments with a `"mod"` stat (see `data/items/attachments/`):

```json
{"id": "ak_compensator", "name": "AK compensator", "category": "attachment", "rarity": "uncommon", "weight": 0.15, "value": 6000,
 "stats": {"mod": {"kind": "muzzle", "fits": ["ak"], "handling": {"vertical_recoil": 1.3, "horizontal_recoil": 0.8}, "modifiers": {"noise": 10}}}}
```

`kind` is one of muzzle, barrel, handguard, foregrip, stock, pistol_grip, receiver, sight, tactical, magazine; `fits` lists gun platforms (`WeaponData.platform`; leave it out for rail parts that fit any gun). A part does what its kind's profile in `data/config/attachment_profiles.json` says; `handling` replaces single entries of it: vertical_recoil, horizontal_recoil, recoil_recovery, base_spread, bloom_per_shot, max_bloom, bloom_recovery, hip_spread, ergonomics, weight. A positive value is a bonus that many times the rarity strength (Common 4% ... Legendary 23%), a negative value a fixed downside in % (ergonomics: points). `modifiers` are fixed effects on velocity, aim_speed, noise, malfunction and durability_loss (percent). `"standard": true` marks the stock part a gun comes with (no effects). Magazines add `"capacity": 30`; sights add `"optic"` at the top of `stats`; parts with rails add `"slots": [{"id": "foregrip", "name": "Foregrip", "kinds": ["foregrip"]}]`.

## Loot

Every container type has its own loot table in `data/loot_tables/` (`crate.tres`, `medical_bag.tres`, `safe.tres`...; loose loot uses `loose_loot.tres`). A roll picks a rarity (base weights Common 55, Uncommon 25, Rare 12, Ultra Rare 5, Epic 2.5, Legendary 0.5) multiplied by the table's own rarity multipliers (a safe boosts rare drops) and the map's (`loot_multipliers` in `data/maps/*.tres`, e.g. Lab Zero doubles Epic and Legendary), the point of interest it stands in (its tier and focus tags, `data/config/pois.json`) and any high-value loot area (`LootZone`), then an item of that rarity the table holds: its item list, categories, item groups (`groups`) and tags (`tags`), all together. Container types (`data/loot_tables/containers/`) set the table, grid size, search time and look; place one in a map with a `LootBox` node. Search timing is in `data/config/raid.tres`. To check the rates, open `scenes/debug/loot_roll_test.tscn` and press **F6** (Run Current Scene): it rolls 10,000 times for each setup and shows expected vs rolled rates, also printed to the Output panel. From a terminal: `godot --path . res://scenes/debug/loot_roll_test.tscn -- --quit --seed=42`.

## Maps

Each raid map is a scene in `maps/` whose root runs `scripts/maps/raid_level.gd` (it bakes the AI navmesh on load and handles death, the raid summary and the map key) and whose `MapInfo` in `data/maps/` points at it (`scene_path`; maps without one show "in development"). Build them from the gray-box nodes in `scripts/world/`, which all show in the editor: `CoverBox` (walls, props and cover: size and bullet material), `Door` (width, `locked` and `key_id`, a name), `Stairs` and `FloorSlab` (upper floors and catwalks), `MapLabel` (area names painted on the floor, landmarks), `LootBox` / `LootPickup` (containers and loose loot), `LootZone` (a box whose `rarity_multipliers` boost every roll inside it), `ScavZone` (AI Scavs spawned at its `Marker3D` children, which they wander between; Scav waves spawn there too), `PointOfInterest` (a named area from `data/config/pois.json`: its tier, focus tags and requirement decide which spawn points inside it spawn and what they roll; `EventSpot_airdrop` / `_truck` / `_cache` markers inside it are where raid events land), `Lever` and `FloorHatch` (a hidden stash), `BossSpawn` (the map's boss from `data/config/bosses.json`, 35% of raids, with guards at its `Marker3D` children), `SpawnPoint`, `ExtractZone` (set its `extract_id`: the rules are the map's row in `data/config/extracts.json`, 1 to 3 extracts with at least one always open; the game warns if the table breaks that) and `PowerSwitch`. Set `ai_pmcs` on the `Raid` node for AI PMCs. A map item is a junk item with `"stats": {"type": "map", "map": "<map id>"}`; the map screen is drawn from the scene itself, so it stays right as the map changes. `maps/factory.tscn` is written by `tools/make_factory_map.py` (`python3 tools/make_factory_map.py .`), but it's an ordinary scene: edit it in the editor, or change the generator and run it again (that overwrites editor changes).

## Notes

- Commit the `.uid` files Godot creates next to scripts and scenes; they keep references stable.
- `.godot/` is a local cache and is git-ignored.
- Placeholder music and UI sounds are generated by `tools/make_placeholder_audio.py` (needs Python, numpy and ffmpeg).
- When you set up an export preset, add `*.json` to its "Filters to export non-resource files" so JSON items are included.
